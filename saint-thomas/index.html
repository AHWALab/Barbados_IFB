Warning colour comes from the Flood Risk Matrix of the Scottish Flood Forecasting Service and the UK Flood Guidance Statement, as applied in Speight et al. (2018): likelihood from the FIM ensemble exceedance grids, impact severity from the depth reached at each receptor. Green is business as usual, yellow be aware, amber be prepared, red take action.

Buildings are drawn at their representative point so several thousand can be read at once. Click any feature for its probabilities and severity.

receptor product

Severity is graded on the depth a receptor reaches: minor from 0.10 m, significant from 0.30 m, severe from 0.70 m. Likelihood is the exceedance probability at that depth, in the bands very low under 20 percent, low 20 to 40, medium 40 to 60, high above 60. A receptor takes the worst matrix cell across its severity specific probabilities. District risk aggregates its receptors and is then read against the same matrix.

Three cycles, not four. The flood inundation run also wrote products for 17 August 15:00, but those rasters are on the Saint Michael window, not Saint Thomas, so they are not sampled onto this receptor stock. The FIM viewer shows that cycle, badged, on its own window.
Scope. The receptor window is the Saint Thomas FIM domain plus a 250 m buffer, so counts describe that window, not the whole parish or the island. Population is the 2010 census total for each enumeration district, disaggregated to buildings by footprint area weighted with the GHS BUILT-C functional class. The likelihood cutoff for the district flags is 0.50.
