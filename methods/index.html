Method and data note

Impact-based flood forecast, Barbados, Saint Thomas pilot

Version for the Barbados training, August 2026 IBF viewer home   The map

1. What this product is

For every forecast cycle in which the flood inundation mapping (FIM) routine issues products, the impact-based forecasting (IBF) routine scores each receptor in the Saint Thomas window against a likelihood by impact matrix and returns a warning colour. The output is a map a forecaster or a responder can act on, together with a table of counts per district. It is produced from the same ensemble the FIM viewer displays, so the two are always consistent with each other.

2. The adopted standard

The product follows Speight et al. (2018), Journal of Flood Risk Management 11:S884 to S901, the Glasgow operational surface water pilot, whose framework is the Scottish Flood Forecasting Service and UK Flood Guidance Statement standard. Warning level is likelihood multiplied by potential impact severity, rendered green, yellow, amber and red for overall risk very low, low, medium and high.

Likelihood

Likelihood comes from the FIM exceedance probabilities, that is the share of the 50 ensemble members whose matched flood scenario reaches a given depth at that pixel. Bands follow the Flood Guidance Statement user guide: very low under 20 percent, low 20 to 40, medium 40 to 60, high above 60, with a 5 percent reporting threshold.

The severity depths are the same for every country in the project, and equal to the first three FIM depth thresholds. A receptor takes the worst matrix cell across its severity specific probabilities, so a low probability of severe flooding is visible as amber rather than disappearing behind a single cutoff. The fourth FIM product, 1.00 m, is sampled onto every receptor as its own column but is not a severity anchor, because the matrix has three impact classes above minimal.

Census population is allocated to buildings by footprint area weighted with the GHS functional class, computed over whole districts so that clipping to the forecast window cannot re-inflate the totals. The enumeration district layer is preferred over the parish layer because it resolves impact at a scale a responder can use, and because intersecting districts cover far less area than whole parishes, which halves the receptor cache build.

Only 1.4 to 4 percent of Overture buildings in the islands carry a subtype, so the functional class raster, not the attribute, does most of the work in separating residential from non residential stock.

Critical facilities

Critical facilities were previously limited to whatever the Overture subtype happened to record, which identified 35 buildings in this window. They now come from the POI layer of an OpenStreetMap extract dated 6 April 2026, which carries an amenity tag on every one of its 1,627 points and polygons. Health, education and civic amenities are mapped onto the project subtype vocabulary and joined to the Overture footprints: a facility inside a building takes that building, otherwise the nearest building within 25 m. Nationally that is 366 facilities, 343 of which match a footprint; in this window it is 51 named facilities and 49 flagged buildings, a gain of 14 over the Overture attributes alone.

The same extract also holds buildings and roads, and they were examined and not adopted. Its 149,553 building footprints are a quarter fewer than the 204,764 in the Overture layer, and 96.5 percent of them carry only building=yes, so a swap would have lost coverage and gained no usable attribute. Its 21,447 road segments are likewise fewer than the 31,312 in use. Places of worship, 257 nationally, are classified as religious rather than critical, because designating a church as a shelter is a national decision and not an attribute of the map data.

4. What the routine does per cycle

  1. Read the four probability grids written by the FIM routine for that cycle.
  2. Build the receptor window from the product raster plus a 250 m buffer, applied in metres after reprojection.
  3. Sample each probability grid onto every building, road segment and district with exact extract maximum semantics.
  4. Grade severity from the depth thresholds, read likelihood from the probability bands, and take the worst matrix cell.
  5. Aggregate to districts through the reporting thresholds, then read the district against the same matrix.
  6. Write a GeoPackage with the three classified layers, a district summary table and a JSON summary.

6. Open items

  • The census population vintage is 2010 for the enumeration districts, while the parish layer carries 2021 Barbados Statistical Service estimates. The two are not mixed in one product; the vintage difference should be resolved with the national partners.
  • Velocity is not available for these cycles, so the flood hazard rating upgrade used in the earlier island wide scenario product is not applied here.
  • Critical facilities are now carried, from the OpenStreetMap extract, but the coverage is what volunteers have mapped rather than an official register. A national list of health facilities, schools and emergency services from the Barbados authorities would replace it and would let the community services thresholds of the standard be applied with confidence.
  • Whether places of worship should count as critical, given their use as shelters in the Caribbean, is a question for the national partners. They are carried as religious and are not currently critical.
  • Road classes are carried but not yet weighted, so a trunk road and a residential street of the same depth take the same colour.
